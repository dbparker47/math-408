    Worksheet Real Number Arithmetic
    

    Let \(A\) be the set of all Cauchy sequences of rational numbers. Let \(\mathbb{R}\) be the set of equivalence classes of \(A\) under the equivalence relation we defined previously.
    🔗

    1.

    Let \(X,Y \in \mathbb{R}\) (so \(X\) and \(Y\) are equivalence classes whose elements are Cauchy sequences of rational numbers). How would you define \(X+Y\text{?}\) Describe it as a process, and make sure that your answer is in \(\mathbb{R}\) as defined above.
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Let \(X,Y \in \mathbb{R}\text{.}\) How would you define \(X \cdot Y\text{?}\) Describe it as a process, and make sure that your answer is in \(\mathbb{R}\) as defined above.
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Suppose that \(\{a_n\}\) and \(\{ b_n \}\) are Cauchy sequences such that \([\{ a_n \}] = [\{2,2,2,2, \ldots \}]\) and \([\{ b_n \}] = [\{ 3,3,3,3, \ldots \}]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    1. What is your guess for \([\{2,2,2,2, \ldots \}] \cdot [\{ 3,3,3,3, \ldots \}]\text{?}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Suppose you use the sequences \(\{ a_n \}\) and \(\{ b_n \}\) as representatives of \([\{2,2,2,2, \ldots \}]$ and $[\{ 3,3,3,3, \ldots \}]\text{.}\) Prove that \([\{ a_n \}] \cdot [\{ b_n \}]\) is the same as in part (a).
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Explain how the addition and multiplication defined in #1 and #2 could potentially be ambiguous.
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    (Not on quiz) Explain what you must assume and what you must prove in order for addition in \(\mathbb{R}\) to be well defined. Then try proving that addition is well defined.
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    (Not on quiz) Prove as many of the field axioms as you can for \(\mathbb{R}\) with addition and multiplication defined above. You may use the fact that \(\mathbb{Q}\) is a field.
    🔗
